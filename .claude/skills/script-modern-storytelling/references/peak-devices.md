# Hooks, peaks, releases and connectors

Each slot in the peak map gets one device. Pick by what the core idea allows, not by what sounds
loud. A device only works when it breaks a real expectation the viewer holds.

## Hooks (about 70/100, 0-3 s, 9 words at most)

| Device | Shape | Example | Fails when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mid-conflict | name the problem as already happening | "Your videos fail because of this one mistake." (from the user's notes) | the mistake is never named, or is obvious |
| You-statement | tell the viewer something true about them | "You don't hate mornings. You hate your first ten minutes." | it is vague enough to fit anyone |
| Mid-reveal | start at the moment the thing is shown | "This is the page nobody reads." (over the image) | the visual does not match the words |
| Result first | show the end, then rewind | "Same camera, same room. One change." | the change is small |
| Contrarian opener | reject the common advice | "Stop posting every day." | the video cannot defend it |
| Specific number | a real figure that surprises | (sourced only) | the number is not checked this session |
| Sharp question | a question the viewer cannot answer yet | "Why does the second video always do better?" | the viewer can answer it, or it is yes/no |

Never open with: "Hey guys", "Welcome back", "Today I'm going to", "In this video", "Let me tell
you", "So,", a name or an introduction, a logo sting, or a slow scene-setter.

**Visual hook:** the first frame must move or show the hook's key word. In rcg that is the first
`stack` or `prompt` on an info-graphics ground, a hero keyword in marketing-pro, a cut-out popping
on a board in mix-media, or a `rcg title --preset slam` in a generic edit.

## Peaks (85-95/100)

| Device | Shape | Expectation it breaks |
|---|---|---|
| Contrast | X vs Y, before vs after, them vs you | "these two are alike" |
| Contrarian take | "But it's not X. It's Y." | the common belief |
| Surprising fact | a sourced number, date or finding | "I know roughly how big this is" |
| Twist / misdirection | set up one reading, then flip it | "I know where this is going" |
| Reframe | the same thing seen from a new side | "I understood the problem" |
| Specificity drop | name the exact thing after talking about it in general | "this is general advice" |
| Stakes raise | "And it gets worse." / what it costs | "this is a small problem" |
| Loop payoff | answer the question opened earlier | "they'll never get to it" |
| Strong visual | a line written for a moment the picture carries | "this is just talk" |
| Bold statement | a short, firm claim the video then backs | "this will be safe and mild" |

Rules:
- Write each peak as its own short sentence, starting a new voice line.
- Put the key word last in its clause. It is where the visual hit lands.
- Never use the same device twice in a row. Over a 60 s video, use at least three kinds.
- A "surprising fact" without a source this session becomes a contrast or reframe instead.

## Releases (30-50/100)

Releases keep the viewer oriented. They are not filler.

- **Explain:** why the peak is true, in one or two sentences.
- **Example:** one concrete case the viewer can picture.
- **Relatable moment:** "You've done this. You open the app, you scroll, you leave."
- **Context:** where this comes from, kept short.

End each release with a **lift** into the next peak: a "but", a question, a stake, or "here's
the catch". Releases in videos of 60 s or less stay at 10 s or less.

## Connectors

| Use | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| But / except / until | conflict, a turn | "It worked. Until the third week." |
| So / therefore / which means | consequence | "So start in the middle." |
| Here's the catch / here's the thing | lift into a twist | |
| Avoid: and then, also, next, another thing, firstly | a list; nothing changes | |

Test: read each pair of sentences aloud with "and then" between them. If it fits, rewrite the
second line so "but" or "so" fits instead, or cut it.

## Open loops

- Open a question in the hook or peak 1. Close it in the payoff.
- In videos of 45 s or more, open a second, smaller loop before closing the first.
- Every loop is closed by the end. An unclosed loop reads as bait.

## Misdirection

Set up the obvious reading, let the viewer predict, then flip it. One per video under 90 s, about
two-thirds in. "Here's the catch" or "But that's not the real problem" are the usual doors. The flip
must be true: a twist that the facts do not support breaks the expectation-vs-reality contract.

## Call to action

Optional. One line after the payoff, tied to the core idea ("Try it on your next video."). No
"smash that like button". Nothing after it.

## Peaks in the edit

A peak in the script is only felt when the picture and sound change with it. Put the biggest
visual change of each stretch on the peak's key word, and keep releases calmer. If every beat gets
a big change, nothing reads as a peak.

| Style | On a peak | On a release |
|---|---|---|
| `style-info-graphics` | a new `ground` (wipe, arc or iris) and the scene's orange word on the peak's key word, with its hit sound | stay on the ground; add parts to the same hero object (`orbit` chips, `stairs` steps) |
| `style-marketing-pro` | write the peak as its own sentence so the planner opens a beat there; check the plan report shows a punch or a hero keyword on it, and note it in the beat sheet if not | base zoom and drift; body captions only |
| `style-split-screen-edit` | a layout change (presenter full frame, or the info clip full frame); questions and numbers tend to trigger one, so check the plan report | the split layout |
| `style-mix-media` | a new board (hard cut) or a large sticker or cut-out pop with its pin or press sound | notes and small pieces added on the same board |
| `style-vox-parallax`, `style-quiet-editorial`, `style-tactile-collage` | a new scene, card or camera move | hold the scene; small additions |
| Generic full workflow | `rcg transition` (`zoom_punch`, `flash_white`, `glitch_punch`), `rcg camera` (`crash_zoom_in`, `face.punch`), `rcg title --preset slam`, a measured SFX hit placed by its crest | `static_shot` or `vc.ken_burns`, music ducked under the voice |

The planner tools decide some cuts on their own (marketing-pro, split-screen). The script cannot
force them; it can make each peak a clean, short sentence so the planner finds it. Read the plan
report and fix the beat sheet where a peak got no change.
