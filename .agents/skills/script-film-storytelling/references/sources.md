# Sources and what was checked

Research for this skill was done on 2026-10-15 with web searches. Most results were secondary
(summaries, blogs, film sites); primary texts (the books) were not read for this skill. Where details
were not confirmed, this file says so. Paraphrase these sources; do not quote them as exact words to
the user or on screen.

| Idea | Source | What was confirmed | Not confirmed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Suspense vs surprise, the bomb under the table | Alfred Hitchcock in François Truffaut's interviews (recorded 1962; book *Hitchcock/Truffaut*, first published 1966-67, revised 1984) | the example and its point (seconds of surprise vs minutes of suspense; surprise is right for a twist), reported by several film sites and David Bordwell's blog; Bordwell notes Hitchcock said the idea was not new | exact wording and page; copies differ |
| Three questions per scene, "every scene must be dramatic", no explaining scenes | David Mamet, memo to the writers of the TV series *The Unit*, reposted widely from March 2010 (Slashfilm, No Film School and others) | the three questions (who wants what, what if not, why now) and the drama-as-pursuit-of-a-goal idea appear in all copies | no primary copy from Mamet or the production was found; wording varies |
| Scenes turn a value (positive to negative or back); "non-event" | Robert McKee, *Story* (1997), and mckeestory.com, "Do your scenes turn?" | the value-turn test, from McKee's own site | the exact phrase "gap between expectation and result" in the book |
| Controlling idea (value + cause) | McKee, *Story* | the two-part definition, from a reprint of a McKee blog post | page-level wording |
| Ranking of reasons for a cut (Rule of Six) | Walter Murch, *In the Blink of an Eye* (1995; 2nd edition 2001) | order emotion, story, rhythm, eye-trace, 2D plane, 3D space, with the weights 51/23/10/7/5/4, consistent across secondary sources; the weights are presented as playful, not measured | the book's exact wording |
| Juxtaposition changes a shot's meaning (Kuleshov effect) | Lev Kuleshov's montage experiments with actor Ivan Mozzhukhin, known mainly from Vsevolod Pudovkin's later account | the general story and that it is widely taught | the date, the exact shots (accounts differ) and whether it replicates; the original footage is lost, and modern replications disagree |
| Pixar storytelling list | 22 tips tweeted by Emma Coats, then a Pixar storyboard artist, in 2011 | the list exists, and it is her notes from colleagues, not an official Pixar rulebook | exact wording and numbering vary between copies |
| Story circle | Dan Harmon, a simplification of Joseph Campbell's hero's journey | the eight labels (you, need, go, search, find, take, return, change), consistent across summaries | Harmon's own wording and when he first wrote it |
| Four-part structure | Kishōtenketsu (ki, shō, ten, ketsu), from Chinese and Japanese poetry and narrative | the four parts; the "without conflict" label is disputed, and works built on it can contain conflict | specific film examples beyond one encyclopedia mention |
| Three-act paradigm | Syd Field, *Screenplay* (1979) | widely cited as the source of the modern three-act teaching | not re-checked in this session (well-known) |
| Feature beat sheet | Blake Snyder, *Save the Cat!* (2005) | widely cited | not re-checked in this session (well-known); only its general ideas are used |
| Writing short films | Pat Cooper and Ken Dancyger, *Writing the Short Film* (3rd ed., Focal Press, 2005; later printings) | the book and its topics (telling a story in images, sound, the main character, dramatic and dialogue strategies) from catalog records | its specific rules; the defaults in `craft.md` section 7 are general short-film practice, not quotes from it |
| Grammar of shots and cuts | Daniel Arijon, *Grammar of the Film Language* (1976) | named as a standard reference | not re-checked in this session; the meanings in `cinema-language.md` are common conventions |
| Short film length | Academy Awards rules for short film categories (oscars.org rule sheets, 91st-97th ceremonies) | a short is 40 minutes or less, including credits | the rule for the newest ceremony was not opened |
| "But / therefore", not "and then" | Trey Parker and Matt Stone, from a talk to a film class, as widely shared | widely credited; also used in `script-modern-storytelling` | the exact words and date |
| Chekhov's gun | advice in Anton Chekhov's letters | widely attributed | the exact wording; it appears in several forms |
| Screenplay format | Fountain, a plain-text markup for screenplays (fountain.io) | in common use | not re-checked in this session |
