# The setups: what each light says

Each entry has five parts. **Says**: the read an audience takes from it. **Light**: where a real light
would be (the light plot draws this). **Quality**: hard or soft, contrast, colour. **Use when** and
**Avoid when**: story moments. **rcg**: the `relight` setup name and what it draws.

The first six come from the user's breakdown (2026-10-11). The other six are standard cinematography
setups added so rcg has a fuller vocabulary. The meanings are common readings, not rules: context,
performance and music can change them. When a setup's read depends on context, it says so.

Azimuth on the plot: 0 = beside the camera, 90 = straight to the side, 180 = behind the subject.
"Camera-left" means the left of the frame.

---

## Front light, `front`: innocence

- **Says:** open, honest, nothing to hide. Youth, trust, a clean slate, a victim before the fall.
- **Light:** a large soft source on or near the camera axis, at about eye height.
- **Quality:** soft, low contrast, the face evenly lit, warm-neutral. Few shadows, so little shaping:
  the face can look flat, which is part of the read (simple, unguarded).
- **Use when:** introducing a likeable or naive character; the "before" in a before/after; testimonials
  that need trust.
- **Avoid when:** the scene needs tension or depth: flat light kills drama.
- **rcg:** a soft warm wash over the face, lower contrast, a mild background drop. Works without a matte.

## Split / side light, `split`: secrecy

- **Says:** a divided self. Something hidden, a double life, a moral choice, mystery.
- **Light:** one hard source at 90 degrees, at eye height. The other side gets no fill.
- **Quality:** hard, very high contrast; the shadow line runs down the nose.
- **Use when:** a lie, a secret, "there's something you don't know about me", a turn to the dark side.
- **Avoid when:** the character is meant to be fully trusted; holding it for long tires the eye.
- **rcg:** a hard half shadow (depth 0.93) through the face centre, turning with head roll; the key side
  defaults to camera-left as in the reference (`side`). Strong with a matte (background to black).

## Low-angle / under light, `under`: threat

- **Says:** danger, menace, the uncanny. Daylight and ceiling lights come from above, so light from
  below reads as wrong. This is the campfire-story or flashlight-under-the-chin "monster light".
- **Light:** a source below the face, close and in front, pointing up.
- **Quality:** usually hard. Shadows climb up the face: the brow and forehead go dark, the chin and the
  underside of the nose catch light.
- **Use when:** the reveal of a villain, a threat, a horror beat, a character losing control.
  Motivated sources: a fire, a phone screen held low, a flashlight, a desk lamp.
- **Avoid when:** you want menace that stays subtle (use `top` or `noir`); it can tip into comedy if held.
- **rcg:** darkness from the top of the head down to the nose, a warm pool on the chin and jaw, less
  colour, a slight green-cold grade.

## Top light, `top`: unreadable

- **Says:** emotional opacity. The eyes sink into shadow, so we cannot read the thoughts; authority,
  interrogation, a closed-off or burdened figure. It can also read as exhaustion or isolation.
- **Light:** a source straight overhead (a single ceiling bulb, a street lamp, high sun).
- **Quality:** hard to medium. The forehead, nose bridge and cheekbones are lit; the eye sockets and the
  area under the nose and chin are dark.
- **Use when:** the character hides what they feel; a boss, a godfather, an interrogator; a confession
  under a bare bulb.
- **Avoid when:** the audience needs to connect with the eyes in that moment.
- **rcg:** a light pool on the crown and forehead, a shadow band over the eyes (placed on the measured
  eye line), shadow under the chin, darker background.

## Blue backlight, `back-blue`: cold

- **Says:** coldness, detachment, distance. An aloof character the audience is not let close to, or
  loneliness. Also night, the moon, a screen glow.
- **Light:** one cool source behind the subject (here behind and a little to the right), no front light.
- **Quality:** a thin bright rim on the hair and shoulders, the face near black, blue cast.
- **Use when:** the emotional temperature drops: a break-up, a betrayal felt, a cold antagonist, a
  character who has shut down.
- **Avoid when:** the line needs the face; keep it short or let captions carry the words.
- **rcg:** needs the matte. The background drops to near black navy, the subject to about 17%
  brightness and 40% saturation, and a directional blue rim comes from the matte edge.

## Balanced three-point, `three-point`: heroic

- **Says:** confidence, control, the hero. Classic "cinematic" polish, balanced but still shaped.
- **Light:** **key** at about 45 degrees to one side and above eye line; **fill** on the other side, softer
  and weaker (lifts the shadows without removing them); **back** light behind on the fill side, which
  draws a rim and separates the head from the background.
- **Quality:** a soft key, moderate contrast, warm.
- **Use when:** the win, the decision made, the expert speaking, the closing statement.
- **Avoid when:** you need the character to feel lost or unsure (drop the back light, deepen the shadows).
- **rcg:** a soft half shadow (depth 0.5) on the fill side, a warm key pool, a moderate background drop,
  and a thin warm rim on the back-light side (with the matte).

## Rembrandt, `rembrandt`: depth

- **Says:** gravity, inner life, a character with a past. A painterly, serious look named after the
  painter's portraits.
- **Light:** a key at about 45 degrees and high, no fill. The far cheek falls dark except a small
  **triangle of light under the eye**, between the nose shadow and the cheek shadow.
- **Quality:** medium-hard, high contrast, warm.
- **Use when:** reflective monologues, a mentor, a reckoning, documentary portraits.
- **rcg:** a half shadow shifted toward the far side with a lit cheek patch (the triangle, as a placed
  ellipse), a warm key pool and grade.

## Loop light, `loop`: approachable

- **Says:** natural, warm, everyday. The friend, the narrator, a relaxed interview.
- **Light:** a soft key about 30-45 degrees off the camera and a little above. The nose shadow makes a
  small loop toward the corner of the mouth. A light fill or a bounce card on the other side.
- **Quality:** soft, low-moderate contrast.
- **Use when:** the neutral baseline of a talking-head edit (amount 0.5-0.7), so stronger setups stand out
  when the story turns.
- **rcg:** a gentle soft shadow on the far side, a soft key pool, a light background drop.

## Butterfly / Paramount, `butterfly`: idealised

- **Says:** glamour, the star, an admired or idealised figure. Classic Hollywood beauty light.
- **Light:** a key straight in front of the face and high above, often with a bounce below the chin.
  It leaves a small butterfly-shaped shadow under the nose and a shadow under the chin.
- **Quality:** soft to medium, cheekbones shaped, the face symmetrical.
- **Use when:** a public persona, an ad, a character seen through an admirer's eyes.
- **rcg:** a central lit pool on the forehead and cheeks, shadow under the chin, fall-off at the sides.

## Noir, `noir`: guilt

- **Says:** moral ambiguity, guilt, a trap closing. Classic film noir: the suspect, the detective at night.
- **Light:** a hard side key through **window blinds**, deep black shadows, a black-and-white look.
- **Quality:** very hard, very high contrast, no colour.
- **Use when:** confession, crime, paranoia, "I shouldn't have done it."
- **rcg:** a hard half shadow, blind stripes on the key side (they scale and turn with the face),
  desaturated almost to black and white, a dark background.

## Dual colour, `dual`: conflict

- **Says:** inner conflict, a choice, a character pulled between two worlds or two people. Warm against
  cool is the usual pair (orange against teal or blue).
- **Light:** two sources of different colour from opposite sides.
- **Quality:** medium-hard, saturated, the centre of the face darker where the two fall off.
- **Use when:** the dilemma, the temptation, a night city scene with neon and street light.
- **rcg:** a warm pool on one side, a cool pool on the other, a cool rim on the cool side (matte), a dark
  background.

## Silhouette, `silhouette`: the unknown

- **Says:** anonymity, a threat not yet revealed, or an iconic figure before the reveal. Also
  universality: the shape can be anyone.
- **Light:** only the background is lit (a bright window, sky, a lit backdrop); nothing on the subject.
- **Quality:** a pure black shape against a bright ground; at most a faint edge light.
- **Use when:** cold opens, "who is this?", the moment before a reveal (cut to front or three-point on the
  name), anonymous testimony.
- **rcg:** needs the matte. The subject goes to black, a bright backdrop glow sits behind it, with a
  faint white edge.

## Arcs that read well (examples)

- **The fall:** front (innocent) -> split (the secret) -> under (the threat) -> back-blue (cold, lost).
- **The rise:** silhouette (unknown) -> top (closed off) -> rembrandt (reckoning) -> three-point (hero).
- **The confession:** loop (everyday) -> noir (the guilty part) -> front (relief, honesty).
- **The choice:** loop -> dual (torn) -> three-point (decided) or back-blue (gave up).

The reference video runs the six core setups in this order: front, split, under, top, back-blue,
three-point. It ends on the hero.
