# How to Predict Your Layoff and Leave Like a Legend

A short film adapted from the user's essay of the same name. Written with the `script-film-storytelling`
skill. Running time about 7 minutes (7 screenplay pages at about a page a minute; re-time after the
first assembly).

The narration uses the essay's own lines wherever it can. Em dashes in the essay were changed to commas
or periods. Names, a few small physical details and three short lines of dialogue were made up for the
film. They are all listed under "What was invented" at the end, so they can be changed or cut.

---

## 1. Premise

- **Protagonist:** DANA (late 30s), a project lead who keeps a paper notebook and a good pen on the desk
  in a company where everyone else types. The name is a placeholder, so rename freely.
- **Want:** to walk out of the layoff in control, with money, proof of their work and their dignity
  intact.
- **Need:** to stop waiting for the company to recognize their value, and claim it themselves.
- **Obstacle:** a company that plans the layoff in secret, a manager who is already writing Dana out,
  and an HR script built for a meltdown.
- **Stakes:** six months of bills, Dana's own story of the work, and the last impression they leave.
- **Dramatic question:** when the meeting comes, will Dana run it, or will it run them?
- **Logline:** When the signs say their job is about to vanish, a project lead has three weeks to get
  their money, files and story in order, so the termination meeting the company scripted for a
  meltdown becomes the meeting Dana runs.
- **Ending (written first):** weeks later, a calendar alert on Dana's phone again, but this one is
  theirs: a dog walk. Dana laughs. The outcome never changed; who holds the calendar did.
- **Controlling idea (never said on screen as a thesis):** you can't control whether you're let go,
  only how you leave. Preparation turns the loss of a job into a show of control.
- **Image system:**
  - **The pen.** The manager takes notes on Dana's work for the first time. In the meeting, Dana takes
    the notes and they watch.
  - **The calendar alert.** It opens the film as a threat and closes it as a choice.
  - **The camera angle.** On the meeting call, Dana decides what the other side sees.

## 2. Shape and structure map

The shape is **three movements** (`structure.md`, shape B) with a cold open. The film opens on the
essay's first image, the "Quick Sync" invite, then jumps back three weeks to show how Dana knew. The
audience waits for the meeting the whole film, which builds suspense.

| Movement | Target | Scenes | Turn |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold open | 0:00-0:30 | 1 | The invite lands. Dana is calm. Why? |
| I. The signs | 0:30-2:15 | 2-4 | Doubt becomes certainty: "My head was on the chopping block." |
| II. The prep | 2:15-3:30 | 5-6 | Fear becomes a plan, and then the system strikes an hour early |
| III. The meeting | 3:30-5:45 | 7 | Dana runs the meeting. The outcome stays the same, but the power has moved. |
| Aftermath | 5:45-7:00 | 8-10 | The lurkers, the friend who froze, and freedom |

The dramatic question is raised at 0:30 (about 7% in) and answered in scene 7, then closed by the final
image.

## 4. Setups and payoffs

| Setup (scene) | Payoff (scene) |
|---|---|
| Paper checklist, face down (1, written in 5) | Turned face up toward the phone camera and checked off with sweeping marks (7) |
| Meredith takes notes on Dana's work for the first time (3) | Dana takes the notes while HR waits; Meredith has nothing in her hands (7) |
| Complaint draft about Meredith, autosaved when access is cut (6) | HR: "the access cut was an accident." V.O.: "I claim it was an accident." (7) |
| LinkedIn draft in a separate doc (5) | Published minutes after the call (8) |
| Priya's tiny wave and sad smile (4) | Dana writes first, and Priya is relieved (9) |
| The "Quick Sync" calendar alert (1) | A calendar alert for a dog walk (10) |
| "They will always wonder how you knew" (V.O., 8) | Answered for the audience by the whole of movements I and II |

## 5. Screenplay

```fountain
Title: How to Predict Your Layoff and Leave Like a Legend
Credit: Adapted from the essay of the same name

FADE IN:

INT. DANA'S HOME OFFICE - MORNING

A laptop chimes. A calendar alert slides onto the screen:

QUICK SYNC - 9:30 AM - 15 MIN - Organizer: Meredith Hale

Under it, Dana's usual Thursday 1:1 sits untouched at 2:00 PM.

DANA (late 30s) studies it. A coffee goes still halfway to their mouth.

DANA (V.O.)
A meeting appears on your calendar. Fifteen minutes. Outside your 1:1. The subject line says "Quick Sync."

Beat.

DANA (V.O.)
Spoiler. You'll be doing neither.

Dana sets the coffee down... and smiles. They slide a sheet of paper out from under the keyboard, face down, and squares it on the desk. [[SETUP: checklist]]

DANA (V.O.)
If this sounds familiar, congratulations. You survived a layoff. If it doesn't, don't be blindsided.

SMASH CUT TO:

TITLE CARD: HOW TO PREDICT YOUR LAYOFF AND LEAVE LIKE A LEGEND

SUPER: THREE WEEKS EARLIER

INT. OFFICE KITCHEN - DAY

A wall screen shows the company org chart. Two boxes at the top have turned grey. No new names fill them.

COWORKERS crowd around a phone, reading an all-staff email. Dana pours water and watches the grey boxes, not the phone.

COWORKER
Both of them? Same day?

COWORKER 2
"No plans to backfill." What does that even mean?

Dana knows. Dana drinks the water.

DANA (V.O.)
It rolls downhill. When two C-suite leaders leave in one day with no plans to backfill, the positions weren't vacated. They were eliminated. Read: waste in the system.

The grey boxes on the screen. [[SETUP: org chart]]

DANA (V.O.)
Your role has been examined. Your fate, decided. The new guard doesn't know you. Only where you fall on the spreadsheet.

INT. MEREDITH'S OFFICE - DAY

MEREDITH (40s), Dana's manager, leans forward across the desk. A legal pad. A pen. Uncapped.

MEREDITH
Walk me through everything. Projects, status, who's on what.

DANA
Everything?

MEREDITH
Everything.

Dana talks. Meredith writes. And writes. She turns a page. [[SETUP: Meredith's pen]]

DANA (V.O.)
The most attentive 1:1 ever. She even took notes. A first.

Dana's face warms. Then it stops warming.

DANA (V.O.)
My first thought was, wow, she finally cares about my work. Then my gut checked itself.

CLOSE ON the legal pad: Dana's projects, in Meredith's handwriting, under a heading Dana can read upside down: HANDOFF.

[[TURN: hope + -> -]]

DANA (V.O.)
It's not interest. It's succession planning.

INT. OPEN OFFICE - DAY

Dana at their desk. PRIYA (30s), an executive assistant, walks toward Dana's row, sees Dana, and takes the long way around the far side of the printers.

From across the floor she gives a tiny wave. A sad smile. She keeps walking. [[SETUP: Priya's wave]]

DANA (V.O.)
People lie. But their energy doesn't.

Later. LENA (40s), an HR partner, stops at Dana's desk with a question about a form. Her voice is soft. Too soft.

LENA
Just... make sure you've got everything you need. Okay?

She's gone before Dana can answer.

DANA (V.O.)
The message was clear. Don't talk to me, I'll cry.

Dana opens a desk drawer: a framed photo, a good mug, a phone charger. Into a tote bag, one by one.

[[TURN: doubt -> certainty]]

DANA (V.O.)
I took the hint. Now it's time to play it smart, not scared.

INT. DANA'S KITCHEN - NIGHT - MONTAGE

-- A spreadsheet: RENT. COBRA. GROCERIES. EMERGENCY. A cell turns green: 6 MONTHS.

DANA (V.O.)
Finances are king. Six months of bills, COBRA and emergencies. And don't bank on severance.

-- Dana on the phone, laptop showing a streaming account.

CUSTOMER SERVICE (V.O.)
(over the phone)
Oh, I'm so sorry. Of course we can pause that for you.

DANA (V.O.)
Funny how strangers can show you more grace than your employer.

-- Dana scrolls old decks, SOPs, a thank-you email from a client. Dana copies a line into a document called WINS.

DANA (V.O.)
Your story is yours to write. Document every win while you still have access.

-- A separate document: a rewritten LinkedIn profile. Dana does not publish it. Dana saves it. [[SETUP: LinkedIn draft]]

-- Dana drags a folder of personal files to the trash, then drags their best work into a shared drive. Name on every file.

DANA (V.O.)
Leave your best work in shared spaces, with your name on it. A quiet, lasting reminder of who set the bar.

-- Pen on paper. A handwritten list: BENEFITS. SEVERANCE. NON-COMPETE. SEPARATION AGREEMENT. PTO PAYOUT. UNEMPLOYMENT. Dana underlines the last one twice. [[SETUP: checklist written]]

END MONTAGE.

INT. DANA'S HOME OFFICE - DAY (MEETING DAY)

8:31 AM on the laptop clock. The "Quick Sync" invite from the opening sits on the calendar.

Dana types a document titled CONCERNS RE: MY MANAGER. Dana is enjoying this.

Mid-sentence, the screen freezes. A grey box:

YOUR SESSION HAS ENDED. PLEASE CONTACT IT.

A small line under the document title: "All changes saved." [[SETUP: complaint autosaved]]

Dana stares. Then laughs out loud, alone in the room.

DANA (V.O.)
IT jumped the gun. An hour early. They couldn't even time that right.

[[TURN: control + -> - -> amused +]]

INT. DANA'S HOME OFFICE - CONTINUOUS (THE MEETING)

9:30. Dana's phone is propped against a mug. Dana joins the call. Two video tiles: MEREDITH, and a man who was not on the invite, PAUL (50s), HR.

DANA
Hang on, trying to set this up. For some strange reason, my laptop doesn't work!

Meredith and Paul exchange a look.

DANA (V.O.)
HR wasn't on the invite. By design. They think they pulled off a masterclass in secrecy. They're expecting a meltdown.

Meredith reads from a screen we can't see.

MEREDITH
As part of a broader restructuring, your role has been eliminated, effective today...

Dana smiles. Nods.

MEREDITH
...this was a difficult decision...

Dana smiles. Nods. And keeps doing it.

Meredith slows. Loses her place. Finds it.

MEREDITH
...and Paul will walk you through next steps.

DANA
(a small chuckle)
Thank you, Meredith.

Meredith's face. She does not know what to do with "thank you." Her tile goes dark.

DANA (V.O.)
Act One. Her look as she left the call is forever etched in my memory.

Paul clears his throat.

DANA (V.O.)
Act Two.

Dana tilts the phone down. Now Paul can see the desk: the paper checklist, face up, and a pen. [[PAYOFF: checklist]]

PAUL
So, regarding your benefits continuation...

Dana makes a long, sweeping check mark. Paul watches it.

PAUL
...and your final paycheck will include...

Dana raises one finger. One moment. Dana writes. Paul stops mid-sentence and waits.

[[PAYOFF: the pen. Meredith took notes on Dana. Now HR waits on Dana's notes.]]

PAUL
...the separation agreement. In order to receive severance, you'll need to sign...

DANA
I will need time for an attorney review.

Beat.

PAUL
Of course.

Another sweeping check.

PAUL
And about your access this morning. That was an accident.

DANA
Of course.

DANA (V.O.)
Earlier that day I'd drafted a complaint about my manager. It auto-saved when they yanked my access.

Dana writes one more line on the checklist.

DANA (V.O.)
I claim it was an accident. [[PAYOFF: complaint]]

[[TURN: power - -> +]]

PAUL
Is there anything you'd like to share before we close?

Dana sets down the pen. Looks straight into the phone.

DANA
Just that I'm proud of the work. It's all in the shared drive. With my name on it.

A frosty smile. It reads: we're done here.

DANA
Goodbye, Paul.

Dana ends the call. Not Paul.

DANA (V.O.)
Does any of this change the outcome? No. But they rehearse for emotional volatility, not accountability. Flip their script, and you flip the power.

Silence in the room. Dana exhales for the first time in the film.

DANA (V.O.)
You don't burn bridges. You simply light the torch on the way out.

INT. DANA'S HOME OFFICE - MOMENTS LATER

Dana opens the saved LinkedIn draft on a personal laptop. Copies. Pastes. PUBLISH. [[PAYOFF: LinkedIn draft]]

Within seconds, a notification: "Meredith Hale viewed your profile."

DANA (V.O.)
As word spreads, some colleagues will check your LinkedIn right away. Give 'em something worth talking about. Let them wonder how you're already three steps ahead.

INT. CORPORATE OFFICE - IMAGINED

(Dana's imagination, played a little too bright, a little too staged.)

Meredith and Paul in a glass meeting room, retracing steps on a whiteboard. Arrows. A question mark circled three times.

MEREDITH
How did Dana know?

PAUL
Who told her?

DANA (V.O.)
Later, you'll enjoy a good laugh imagining the post-meeting conversation. The retracing of steps. The blame shifting. The spiraling.

SMASH CUT BACK TO:

INT. DANA'S HOME OFFICE

Dana, grinning at nothing.

DANA (V.O.)
They will always wonder how you knew.

INT. DANA'S LIVING ROOM - WEEKS LATER

Dana's phone, a stack of messages: "So sorry." "Their loss." "Coffee soon?" Some names never appear.

DANA (V.O.)
Some people reach out. Some go silent. It's not you, it's them. Some just freeze.

Dana scrolls to PRIYA. No messages since the wave. Dana types: "Hey. I'm good. Hope you are too."

Three dots. Then:

PRIYA (TEXT)
I didn't know what to say. Thank you for writing first.

[[PAYOFF: Priya's wave]]

Dana reads it twice. Puts the phone face down, the way the checklist once lay.

[[TURN: hurt - -> grace +]]

EXT. PARK - MORNING

A big, scruffy dog drags Dana down a path. Dana is laughing, nearly losing a shoe.

Dana's phone buzzes. A calendar alert slides up, the same sound as the opening:

WALK BISCUIT - 9:30 AM - 45 MIN - Organizer: Dana

Dana looks at it. Dana's calendar now.

DANA (V.O.)
Rejection is redirection. The drama is over, and you are free.

The dog pulls. Dana lets it.

[[PAYOFF: calendar alert]]

FADE OUT.

TAG - DANA, TO CAMERA

Dana, on the park bench, dog's head in their lap. Speaks to us directly.

DANA
If you've been through a layoff, share your story in the comments. It might be exactly what someone else needs to hear today.

The dog sighs.

DANA
And if this resonated, follow for more tales on surviving and outsmarting corporate theater. Thanks for watching.

CUT TO BLACK.
```

## 7. Checks (from the skill)

- [x] **Logline:** it names a protagonist, a want, an obstacle and stakes.
- [x] **Dramatic question:** raised at 0:30 (about 7% in), answered in scene 7, and closed by scene 10.
- [x] **Scene turns:** every scene turns a value (column "Value" in the scene cards).
- [x] **Links:** every link between scenes is "but" or "therefore".
- [x] **Climax by choice:** Dana causes the climax through choices: the prep, the silence, the pen,
      "attorney review", and ending the call. No luck solves it. (IT's early cut is a coincidence that
      makes things harder, not easier.)
- [x] **Setups and payoffs:** every setup pays off (table in section 4). One question is left open on
      purpose: the company never learns how Dana knew.
- [x] **No stated feelings:** the dialogue never names a feeling. Priya's text is the closest. It is
      about her own freezing, which is behavior, not Dana's feeling.
- [x] **Small cast:** three main locations (home office, office, park) and five speaking parts (Dana,
      Meredith, Paul, Lena, Priya), plus the coworkers and a voice on the phone.
- [x] **Narration adds something:** it carries Dana's thoughts, the rules of the game and the irony. It
      never describes the shot. Where the picture does the work (Meredith's face, the frosty smile), the
      narration stays silent.
- [x] **First and last images:** the calendar alert, threat at the start and choice at the end.
- [ ] **Length:** about 7 minutes, which sits at the top of the 3-7 minute budget for 10 scenes. Not yet
      timed. If it runs long, cut scene 8's imagined room first, then trim the montage.
- [x] **Real people:** none are named. The film is told from the essay's first-person account only.

## 8. What was invented for the film (change or cut any of it)

- **Names:** Dana, Meredith Hale, Paul, Priya, Lena, and Biscuit the dog. The essay names no one. Its
  manager is "she" and its two friends in the planning are "two women".
- **Gender:** Dana's gender is left open (they/them in the action lines). Cast as you like.
- **Small details:** the "HANDOFF" heading on Meredith's pad, the coworkers' kitchen lines, Lena's line
  "make sure you've got everything you need", the text messages, and the meeting times (8:31, 9:30).
- **Dana's closing words to HR:** "I'm proud of the work. It's all in the shared drive. With my name on
  it." The essay says it is your choice how much to say, so this line is a placeholder for what you
  actually said.
- **Priya's reply:** the essay says the friend "seemed relieved that I made the first move". The text
  wording is made up.
- **The imagined HR scene:** it stages what the essay says to imagine. It is played as Dana's fantasy,
  not as fact.
- **Left out:** the essay's paragraph on negotiating terms, side hustles, and the company-wide email
  naming names. They could return as one V.O. line each if you want them.
- **Not used:** the Medium subscribe text in the pasted essay.
- **Advice in the narration:** the lines on severance, COBRA, unemployment and attorney review are the
  author's own experience as written in the essay. They are not checked legal or financial advice, and
  the film does not present them as such.
